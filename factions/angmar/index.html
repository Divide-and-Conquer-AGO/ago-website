Witch-Realm of Angmar

Description

“The Black Rider flung back his hood, and behold! he had a kingly crown; and yet upon no head visible was it set. The red fires shone between it and the mantled shoulders vast and dark. From a mouth unseen there came a deadly laughter. “Old fool!” he said. “Old fool! This is my hour. Do you not know Death when you see it? Die now and curse in vain!” And with that he lifted high his sword and flames ran down the blade.” - Witch-king of Angmar

Campaign Features

  • Witch King
    • Conquer Eriador and herald the return of the Witch-king to his rightful domain in Angmar. Capture Fornost, Imladris, and hold Carn Dûm. Like other Nazgûl, he will respawn after 15 turns if killed.
  • Sauron’s Will (V3)
    • Capture historical Numenorean settlements such as Fornost and Annuminas to recieve custom Black Numenorean generals and reinforcements from Mordor
  • Great Plague (V3.2)
    • Once the Witch-king of Angmar has returned and the Dark Lord Sauron has the One Ring, you can summon plagues across Middle-earth to cripple other factions and destroy their populations
  • Northern Alliance (V3.2)
    • Ally with Gundabad to gain the ability to recruit Snow-Orcs from your orc barracks or conquer Mt. Gundabad to recieve much more.
  • Recruitable Bodyguards (V3.2)
    • You can recruit Witch-Knights from the Meeting Hall and Gram War-chiefs from the Fighter’s Pit.

Huskarls are found in very small groups, typically as personal guards and trusted warriors of a local Creoth Yurol, worthy of such position through fighting prowess and personal loyalty. In this current era however things have changed.

These Huskarls call themselves Men of Rhudaur and hold no loyalty to the Creoth or local Clans, instead answering to the enforcers of a resurgent Angmar and claim right to the former lands of Rhudaur's old Kingdom. To any hilltribesmen or Creoth warrior it becomes clear that these men are outlanders, not born in Rhudaur itself but rather brought in from the outside.

When Agandaûr set out to restore the Witch Realm his servants spread word of his intention and his march began to draw wicked men, lured by promises of glory and spoils to be taken in the North and made eager by the thought of war against the Dúnedain. These were warriors, mercenaries, outcasts and brigands who were made to believe in the cause of Angmar’s revival. To light their greed they were promised the land of Rhudaur itself and enforce their claim with he heavy axes they wield in battle.

And so Agandaûr, cunning in his leadership, created a great host that would transform that old and broken land into a new state — claimed by the strongest and ruled by the worthy. It would be from this great host that Angmar would create their professional army, those who survived the coming conquest of Rhudaur would rise to join the dark iron legions that Agandaûr envisioned. The foundation for this new army would be laid upon the bloodied ground of Rhudaur.

The Darkblades learnt long ago that the haughty and proud Dúnedain are slow to draw swords and quick to underestimate and it is in the chaos of melee that they shine. They quickly silence all but the most elite of enemies and pass over the battlefield as a great shadow of death. The Darkblades were the Witch-king's pride and now, over a thousand years later, they still slay in his name.

The Northern Dúnedain and Elven Remnants of Eriador both hold reputations as legendary marksmen. They endure a brutal strengthening regime so that they can pull their massive bows back for maximum range and are then shot by Orcs with training arrows, sometimes real arrows if the instructor deems it.

While there is loyalty to ones fellow troops within the Iron Crown is common, there is so such feeling for any of the lower ranks. These men will not hesitate to carry out orders to fire on their own troops if it means dealing damage to an engaged foe. Unflinching in the face of enemy fire, unwavering in their loyalty to their commanders, these troops become honed into a deadly force any Angmarim General can depend upon.

The revival of Angmar has been realized within this core of battle hardened troops. Worthy of being called soldiers, these men have been selected for their skill in battle but more importantly their ability to follow orders and function effectively as a phalanx. Trained under the relentless instruction of the Inquisitors and their Angmarim commanders these men understand their success rests on their ability to meet the enemy as one,Each unit is expected to hold its ground against any odds and collective punishment for cowardice and disobedience encourages a robust sense of responsibility within each soldier. Thus the Iron Crown Pikes form an unassailable center of strength for the army.

Iron in the North is not of high quality and the Angmarim must rely on case hardening, packing their armor with charcoal and bone and sealing it in clay before heating it. Their blades, axes and pikes are folded during forging over and over to eliminate the impurities of the iron. The result is a hardened and darkened metal seen on the Iron Crown troops.

The process is a fitting analogy for the Northern Shadow's war against Eriador: ruthless determination combined with methodical strikes and the resolve to see their task finished despite all hardship. The Iron Crown Army is built to be the lasting power in Eriador and ensure Angmar never falls again.

Worthy of being called soldiers, these men have been selected for their skill in battle and their ability to follow orders and function effectively as a cohort. The Ironcrown army is intended as a direct counter to any disciplined force in middle earth, with its Swordsmen expected to cut through any foe be it Man, Elf, Dwarf or otherwise.

After selection each soldier is assigned to a unit that undertakes training in cohesive unit tactics and physical conditioning. Each new recruit is tested against Orcs and men, typically slaves condemned to death, and their brutality is measured against their comrades. While each soldier has proven to be a worthy warrior, it is during training that they are fully realized as the harbingers of Sauron's Northern Shadow. Angmarim officers and inquisitors oversee the indoctrination of new recruits, selecting them from among the most loyal of the Rhudauric forces and the wicked outlanders who joined Angmar's reclamation.

Discipline is enforced through harsh example, any show of cowardice or mercy is punished with decimation where every third man in the cohort is lashed until bloody and ones guilty of fear are painfully executed by the Inquisitors. Thus the Iron Crown Army does not turn away from its duty to destroy the Northern Realms, to do so is unthinkable.

Life in the far north is unrelenting, and only those who call it home understand its merciless grip. What a Dalian might shiver at, or even an Angmarim might curse, the Lossoth dismiss with disdain. They wander the vast, featureless expanse of the Forodwaith, where snow stretches endlessly toward a dim horizon beneath oppressive, cloud-laden skies. What prey they stalk in that frozen expanse, none from the south can say, but their homes, fashioned from the bones of creatures large enough to Dwarf a noble’s hall, whisper of the monstrous quarry hidden in the white nothingness.

The Lossoth are the true rulers of the north, after a fashion, and their blood is as cold as the lands that sustain them. They lived here long before the Númenóreans carved their fleeting kingdoms into the stone and woods, and when the last ruler of Arthedain drowned in the Bay of Forochel, their fragile realm collapsing into ruin, the Lossoth paid it no heed. They continued their hunt across the tundra, undisturbed.

To endure in such desolation is to embody it. These men are not merely hunters—they are killers forged by hardship and brutal necessity. Their hooked arrows are tipped with poisons that bring down man and beast alike, from lumbering walruses to the stranger creatures of the north. Their layered armor, stitched from fur and sinew, seems strange and cumbersome to the untrained eye, but while others freeze to death beneath the bitter winds, the Lossoth endure.

To wage war in snow and ice is to fight on their terms, and any commander with wisdom will seek their spears and arrows. But those who deal with the Lossoth would do well to remember—they are not men of hearth and warmth. They are born of the cold, their hearts as stark and unyielding as the frozen plains they call home.

These men are the most experienced and battle-ready of the Icewalkers. They have endured many a freezing night by the side of their chief and protected their tribe from beast and man alike. While the population of the Lossoth people is scattered, hard times call for desperate measures and often times taking the food of your fellow tribe is easier than finding prey in the vast wilderness of Forodwaith.
