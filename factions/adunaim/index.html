Ar-Adûnâim

Description

“The great cape and land-locked firth of Umbar had been Númenórean land since days of old; but it was a stronghold of the King’s Men, who were afterwards called the Black Númenóreans, corrupted by Sauron, and who hated above all the followers of Elendil. After the fall of Sauron their race swiftly dwindled or became merged with the Men of Middle-earth, but they inherited without lessening their hatred of Gondor.” - City of Umbar’s past

Campaign Features

  • Free Start
    • Starting as a horde, you may choose where to establish your first settlement in Middle-earth
  • Forced Conscription
    • Forcibly enscript the Free Peoples to fight in your armies as you conquer their lands with custom skins for over 15 units
  • Rebuild the Pillar of Umbar (V3)
    • Rebuild the Pillar of Umbar in defiance of Sauron, signalling to your kinsmen you have established a foothold in Umbar. Provides a custom general and reinforcements from the South
  • Randomized AI colony start (V3)
    • In addition to their preset auto-expansion settlements, the AA also recieve a random coastal settlement (Toggleable, AI only)
  • Traitorous Generals
    • By capturing key settlements in Middle-earth and constructing a conscription camp you can convince various characters to join your cause
  • Ring Script
    • You cannot destroy the ring or bring it to Sauron

Unlike the flamboyant labels christened and carried by lesser men among the Ar-Adûnâim, the Naru n’Aru hold a simple, solemn name. They are the King’s Men. Whether skewered on blade-tip, beaten by shield or crushed beneath the weight of their ever-forward march, all who stand against them fall. No man may threaten their King and live, nor stand in his presence unwatched by guarded eyes. Clad in the black of their more common brethren and gilded in the gold of the King, they are unmistakeable, gleaming upon the battlefield like a shining instrument of death.

These sworn-soldiers serve as elite bodyguards, armed with wicked poleaxes and a will to kill without mercy any and all who would see their Lord harmed. They are a cut above the standard fighting companies, hand-picked for their role by the Lord who commands them. Only the best among the Naru n’Aru may hope to serve among their number, and only from these lucky few do even fewer hear a sacred, higher call.

The halberd is a weapon greatly favored by the King. It is an effective weapon with which to attack or defend, and is carried by many a sentry and household guard at home. However, in the hands of the youngest and greenest of the Arunvênîr, it is a weapon of pure aggression. These eager men are first to fight and first to die, charging the enemy with all the zealotry and fervor of youth hellbent on glory. Only a foolish foe would deem them a lacking threat, and any fool who did would soon discover the error of this assumption. Their oath is one of courage and hope - to fight with bravery, to live with honour, and to die in glory.

Not all among the ranks of the Naru n’Aru do so unwaveringly. There are those who fall short of their oaths, who flee or fail in battle, disgracing themselves, their King and his colors. But not all who fail are traitors. Many are good soldiers faced by impossible odds, who fail not by the weakness of their own heart or hand, but by the strength and cunning of another. The King is utterly without tolerance for failure, but he is not without an eye for nuance and circumstance. Those men who swear themselves true of spirit and steadfast of soul may again earn the trust of their beloved King. Yet they may not do so without proof of their devotion. A man must prove beyond question his willingness for bravery, and acceptance for sacrifice. Thus are formed the Dala an’Nagalu; ‘those who dare to die’. Distinguished by their long scarlet sashes emblazoned with the gold of the King, these men fight so that that very gold may once again adorn their armor in the grave. With greatswords in hand and a sureness of heart surpassed only by those they have failed, they are a force more vicious and brutal than any other fielded in the army of the King. They have but a single purpose on the field of battle - to find redemption in the deaths of their enemies, or to die with honour trying.

In preparation for the Great Journey to Amatthâni, the Golden King Ar-Pharazôn knew that He would need the finest ships that Arda had ever seen. He tasked the great shipwrights with constructing these new behemoths, and after years of toil, their work did not disappoint. Against the power of The Destroyer, even their might was not enough, and all but one of these vessels were obliterated. Her name was the Azrazâir, or 'Sea-longing', a lone survivor which fled the horror of the Akallabêth to sail south in stricken silence. Yet in the darkness of the Great Sea, so too did this brave vessel fall. Only by her strength and sacrifice did a few shaken survivors reach the coast to tell their darkest tale; the death of their King, the annihilation of their fleet, and most terrible of all, the sinking of Anadûnê and the death of all they loved. In reverence to this holy ship, the last symbol of the Golden King, the greatest warriors of the Black Fleet have taken up the name Azrazâir as their own. Though lesser soldiers may cheer Her name in battle, only these men truly carry it. With every man slain by their blades, they honour His might, and with every battle they survive, they honour Her sacrifice.

These marines are greater fighters than all others who sail beside them. At sea they serve as bodyguards, protecting the captains and admirals of their fleets with steel nerves and iron resolve. On land they serve as blocks of heavy infantry, well-armored in scale hauberks and armed with deadly handaxes. Their javelins are heavy and tipped with steel points, more than capable of killing a man even through light armor. Only a foolish commander would disregard the warfighting prowess of these soldiers, and only a madman would charge their line with confidence.

While the true majesty of Anadûnê has been lost, remnants of its height remain. One such remnant is that of the steelbow, a weapon of divine power and furious might. However, over time, even remnants of a God may fade. Such is the case with the steelbows of the Ar-Adûnâim. These ancient weapons are forged by the smiths of the House of Fallen Leaves, and carried into battle by their fearless scions. The arrows loosed by these mighty weapons, themselves forged of slender steel, are cut with special channels along their length so they may whistle as they fly. Though weaker than their ancestors of old and carrying steelbows far less powerful, these men are no less keen of sight and sharp of mind. Their range and power are legendary, and their accuracy is absolute. Many a foolish man has believed himself safe beyond the range of wooden bows, only to die in shock, skewered with needles of blood-stained steel.

Outside the ranks of the Naru n’Aru and those few who share the blood of the King, none may wear the color gold - save for these men. Legends tell of a dark moment in ages past, in the midst of a great war against a long forgotten foe. A great battle was fought amidst the ruined city of Sahan’dahar, jewel of a forgotten kingdom in a long lost land. In the heart of this city, where men fought and died by the thousands, King Ar-Karikând found himself to stand alone against the enemy. It was in this most dire moment that a lone company of bowmen proved themselves more steadfast than any other, saving their King from certain death and earning without question the right to wear his gold.

When Ar-Gimilkhâd led his hordes against the Angmarim, it was the Northguard who resisted the longest. With iron determination they fought the King's Men; though in the end, their efforts proved futile. The Old Witch-realm was conquered. Ar-Gimilkhâd, however, acknowledged their prowess and valour and offered them a choice. They could either leave in peace or join the ranks of the Adûnâim. Most declined, still believing that serving the Iron Crown was the only true way in life, but some accepted. These soldiers were formed into an elite battalion, subjecting themselves to ruthless military drills. They were equipped with the finest Númenórean armour and brutal maces.
Today, these hardened veterans form the backbone of the Angmarim conscription forces. Though having become almost Númenórean in discipline and equipment, they retain their savage lust for battle.
